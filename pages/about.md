I began my formal scientific journey as a Physics major at the University of Southern California (USC), where I participated in several research laboratories. Near the end of my undergraduate degree–and during the Covid pandemic– I had the unique opportunity to work remotely with the Moffitt Cancer Center’s Integrated Mathematical Oncology Lab in Tampa, Florida, which applies math to medicine in a similar way as math applied to physics. Seeing this versatility and potency in mathematical technique, I supplemented my Physics degree with Applied Mathematics and graduated from USC in 2022.

I continued investigating this novel research perspective by starting a masters in Mathematical Biology at the University of St Andrews. Following this program, I moved back to California, and began a PhD in Mathematical, Computational, and Systems Biology in 2023, expecting to begin a career in interdisciplinary science. However, I decided early in the program to transfer and attempt a PhD in Mathematics instead. The foremost *logical* reason that my value as a researcher appears greater when specializing in a particular analytical mode of scientific inquiry.

These varied experiences have specially prepared me to communicate across disciplines while retaining rigorous training in the mathematical sciences. My academic hope is to develop rigorous mathematical insights while simultaneously —and synergistically— supporting theory development in the natural sciences.
