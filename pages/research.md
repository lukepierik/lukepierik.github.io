## Previous Projects

### Second-Order Effects of Chemotherapy Pharmacodynamics and Pharmacokinetics on Tumor Regression and Cachexia

Until 2025, my research focus had been in mathematical biology, specifically oncology and population dynamics, and in Spring 2024, I <a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11538-024-01278-0" target ="_blank">published my first paper</a> alongside my mentor <a href="https://jeffreywest.github.io/index.html" target="_blank">Jeffrey West</a> at the Moffitt Cancer Center in Tampa, Florida. Here we completed some analysis and simulations for different deterministic models of cancer proliferation and repression to determine when a tumor will respond positively (or negatively) to increased treatment variability. We characterized the benefit gained from treatment variability through a so-called “fragility metric” and found that higher treatment variability benefited smaller tumors and lean mass preservation. This result contributes to a growing body of literature challenging the standard-of-care procedure which uniformly delivers a predetermined maximum tolerated drug dose to a patient who is experiencing cancer, and therefore may help promote new therapies treating a patient’s unique disease profile.

### Modeling the Emergence of Flatwing Mutations for the Field Cricket *Teleogryllus oceanicus*

In September 2023, I completed my Master's Thesis at the University of St Andrews under the supervision of <a href="https://alexanderjstewart.org/index.html" target ="_blank">Alexander Stewart</a>. This unpublished work considers a modern example of convergent evolution on the Hawaiian islands where field crickets (*Teleogryllus oceanicus*) adopt a "flatwing" mutation, which inhibits the ability for male flies to attract females, but has the advantage of evading predation by local flies. Despite extensive measurements of local cricket populations, no rigorous investigation of the tradeoffs between female attraction and fly evasion existed. This work developed a novel ecological-genetic model that predicts conditions for the mutant and non-mutant crickets to coexist with the flies given certain basic parameters. This model therefore hypothesizes bounds on biological parameters within the Hawaiian cricket-fly system and offers a means to explore other enviroments where cricket-fly coexistence is possible.
