Until 2025, my research focus had been in mathematical biology, specifically oncology and population dynamics, and in Spring 2024, I published my <a href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11538-024-01278-0" target ="_blank">first paper</a> alongside my mentor <a href="https://jeffreywest.github.io/index.html" target="_blank">Jeffrey West</a> at the Moffitt Cancer Center in Tampa, Florida. Here we completed some analysis and simulations for different deterministic models of cancer proliferation and repression to determine when a tumor will respond positively (or negatively) to increased treatment variability. We characterized the benefit gained from treatment variability through a so-called “fragility metric” and found that higher treatment variability benefited smaller tumors and lean mass preservation. This result contributes to a growing body of literature challenging the standard-of-care procedure which uniformly delivers a predetermined maximum tolerated drug dose to a patient who is experiencing cancer, and therefore may help promote new therapies treating a patient’s unique disease profile.
